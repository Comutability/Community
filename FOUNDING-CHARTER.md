# Comutability Founding Charter

**Version:** 1.1  
**Type:** Independent, community-led, open-source initiative  
**Status:** Approved by founder on 28 October 2026  
**Established:** 2026

## Our mission

To bring people together to create genuinely useful, accessible and open-source software, using artificial intelligence to support creativity, collaboration and development.

We exist to solve real problems and make technology more accessible to everyone.

## Our vision

A global community where anyone, regardless of their technical knowledge, background or financial circumstances, can help create, improve and benefit from open-source software.

## Our ten founding principles

### 1. Community before organisation

Our strength comes from the people who participate. We encourage collaboration, mutual respect and shared decision-making.

### 2. Open source for everyone

Our released software will use recognised open-source licences, allowing people to use, study, modify and redistribute it freely.

### 3. AI and humans working together

AI supports research, programming, testing, documentation and other activities. Human contributors and maintainers retain responsibility for decisions and releases.

### 4. Anyone can contribute

Contributions can include ideas, code, testing, documentation, translations, accessibility improvements, design and community support.

You do not need to be a professional developer to participate.

### 5. Accessibility and inclusion

Our tools should be approachable and usable by people with different abilities, skills and circumstances.

### 6. Privacy, security and freedom

We respect people's privacy, promote secure development practices and avoid unnecessary restrictions or vendor lock-in.

### 7. Quality over quantity

We aim to create software that works, is properly tested, is documented and can be maintained over time.

### 8. Transparent governance

Project priorities, significant decisions, maintainer responsibilities and community rules should be documented openly.

### 9. Independence and sustainability

The community is not organised primarily to generate profit.

Voluntary donations, sponsorships, grants and other transparent funding arrangements may support infrastructure, maintenance and development.

Funding must not automatically grant control over community decisions.

### 10. Knowledge should be shared

We encourage learning, experimentation and public documentation so others can build on our work.

## How our community operates

The community will organise development through publicly accessible repositories, discussions and documented contribution processes.

Individual projects may have dedicated maintainers and working groups.

The founder will initially coordinate the community's direction and infrastructure, with governance designed to support greater community participation as the initiative grows.

Major decisions should be recorded transparently, with clear ways for contributors to offer feedback and raise concerns.

## What we create

Our community can develop software across any useful category, including:

- Everyday utilities and productivity tools.
- Artificial intelligence and automation.
- Privacy, security and decentralisation.
- Self-hosting and infrastructure.
- Education and accessibility.
- Creative tools and entertainment.
- Developer tools and experimental technologies.

We are not limited to any one category.

## Our development approach

**Identify a problem → Discuss ideas → Design → Build → Test → Review → Release → Maintain**

AI can assist throughout this process, but releases must have appropriate human oversight.

## Our founding promise

We believe useful technology should be accessible to everyone.

We will build openly, share knowledge, welcome contributions and use AI responsibly to help turn good ideas into working software.

**Built together. Open to everyone.**
